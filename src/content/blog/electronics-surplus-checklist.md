## Make the lot understandable before discussing an offer

An electronics stock enquiry should identify **models, quantities, tested or untested status, known faults, accessories and stock location**. These details help a buyer understand the opportunity beyond a headline brand name or original retail value.

Untested does not mean working, and it does not automatically mean faulty. It describes an important uncertainty. Preserve that distinction when preparing your stock list.

## Identify models and variants precisely

Two devices that look similar may have different specifications, connections or accessories. Record the model and variant rather than relying only on a general description such as “wireless speakers.”

Use consistent unit counts. If a row represents a mixed carton, explain what is known about the contents. If a count is approximate, mark it accordingly.

Keep detailed serial-number records available where appropriate, but agree a suitable channel before sharing extensive device identifiers or commercially sensitive files.

## Explain what testing actually means

“Tested” is useful only when the buyer understands what was checked. A power-on check is different from a broader functional assessment.

Record the scope of testing, when it happened and whether it covered all units or a sample. List known faults without assuming every device has the same issue.

If there has been no testing, state that clearly. Do not upgrade an untested lot to “working returns” because a few units looked clean. Equally, avoid treating an entire lot as defective because one sample failed.

The aim is an accurate description, not a more attractive label.

## Separate accessories and cosmetic condition

A product may be usable while missing its original charger, cable, remote or other component. Identify what is included and what is absent.

Describe the outer packaging separately from the product. A worn box and a damaged device are different issues. Photos should show representative units, their packaging and any important exceptions.

Where products are sold as sets, explain whether each set is complete. This helps avoid misunderstandings about what “100 units” actually means at handover.

## Resolve account access and business data

For devices that hold data or connect to user accounts, the stock owner should follow the manufacturer’s transfer instructions. Data removal, account removal and activation locks are distinct questions.

For example, [Apple’s guidance on preparing devices for transfer](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/109511) explains steps relating to personal information and account access. Use the corresponding official instructions for the actual products in your lot.

Do not include customer information in a public inventory enquiry. If a device still contains business data or remains locked to an account, disclose that status before discussing handover.

## Keep reusable inventory and discarded waste distinct

This checklist concerns inventory offered for purchase. Equipment discarded as waste and damaged batteries require separate handling considerations.

Describe any battery damage or other known safety issue before discussing collection. Do not mix it into an ordinary stock description or assume it can travel with the rest of the lot.

You can open the conversation without completing a long technical report. Share the product category, approximate quantities, tested or untested status and location. Mention any known faults, restrictions or missing accessories.
